Market Narrative

Where the Fundamentals Point — and Where They Don't

Healthy & Stable Markets

The strongest multifamily fundamentals concentrate in the northeast Phoenix–Scottsdale corridor — Paradise Valley, the Scottsdale interior, and communities along the Loop 101 — where household incomes reach $200,000–$325,000 and rent-to-income ratios run just 9–13%. Central Phoenix zip codes near Midtown and the Camelback corridor add a different angle: lower incomes but renter shares of 75–85%, creating deep tenant pools and strong re-tenanting velocity. The Southeast Valley — Gilbert, east Chandler, south Tempe — rounds out the investable tier with rising incomes and growing young professional demand.

Stressed & Distressed Markets

The most challenged markets cluster in the western and southwestern corridors — parts of Glendale along Glendale Avenue, southwest Phoenix, Apache Junction, and Sun City. These areas share modest household incomes alongside rent-to-income ratios already near or above the 25–30% healthy threshold, leaving little room for rent growth without triggering turnover. Sun City is particularly constrained: rents are high relative to income, and the owner-dominated, retiree-skewed demographic means the addressable renter pool is structurally thin.

Methodology

How the Index is Built

30%
Income Strength
2025 Average Household Income. Higher-income zip codes sustain stronger rents and more stable NOI over time.
25%
Renter Market Depth
Renter share of total housing units. A deeper renter pool reduces lease-up and re-tenanting risk.
25%
Rent Level
2025 Median Monthly Contract Rent. Higher rents signal pricing power and market depth.
20%
Affordability Ratio
Ratio of monthly median rent to monthly average household income — scored in reverse, so a lower ratio scores higher. Above 33% signals tenant stress and turnover risk.

Each metric is min-max normalized to a 0–1 scale, then combined using the weights above. The composite is rescaled to a 0–100 index where 100 = strongest market in the dataset (Paradise Valley) and 0 = weakest (Lukachukai).

Exclusions: Zip codes with population under 500 were excluded due to statistically unreliable estimates and insufficient market size. Three additional zip codes with missing housing or rent data were also removed.
